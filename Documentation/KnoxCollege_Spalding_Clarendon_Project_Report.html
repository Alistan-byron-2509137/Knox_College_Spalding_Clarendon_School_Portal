2. Background of Knox College

Knox College is a co-educational high school in Spalding, Clarendon, for day pupils and boarders. It opened in 1947 and is named after the Scottish reformer John Knox. It was co-founded by Rev. Dr. Lewis Davidson and Mr. David Bent, and it stands on a hilltop campus of about 100 acres.

The school belongs to the Knox complex of schools, together with the Neighbourhood Early Childhood Institute, Knox Junior School and Knox Community College. The United Church in Jamaica and the Cayman Islands owns and administers the complex. Knox College is a grant-aided secondary school and public sources describe it as the only co-educational boarding school at the secondary level in Jamaica.

3. Why I chose Knox College

I attended Knox College for five years as a boarder, and it became a big part of my childhood. Living on campus meant school was home, routine and community. I studied IT, Electrical, Mathematics, English Language, English Literature, Spanish and French, and I got to know school leaders such as our Principal, Mr. Bourne, and our Vice Principal, Mrs. Platt.

Mr. Brown taught me Mathematics, Miss Black taught me English Language, Miss Williams taught me English Literature, and Miss Gray taught me IT. In every class I was the youngest student, so I made it a habit to give each lesson my best. Along the way I served on a committee on the school board and was nominated for Head Boy. I came second, and I learned that trying your best and serving others matters more than any title.

Knox College’s motto, to strive to serve and not to yield, still guides me as I study Computing. I chose the school because I know it from the inside. That helped me design a portal around real school life: classes, boarding, teachers, grades and tuition.

4. Project overview

The Knox College School Portal is a front-end web application made with HTML5, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript. There is no server and no framework. The browser’s localStorage acts as the database, so records stay after the page is refreshed and every page reads the same data. The login session uses sessionStorage.

PagePurpose
index.htmlHome page with school facts, latest announcements and links to the three portals
about.htmlHistory, motto, academics, student life, achievements, facilities, admissions, teacher directory and my Knox story
contact.htmlSchool address, phones, map and a message form using HTML5 validation (required, pattern, minlength)
login.htmlOne login page that sends each role to its own dashboard; sample accounts and limits are explained
admin-dashboard.htmlStudent CRUD, student IDs, classes and semesters, subjects, grades, tuition ledger, payments, announcements, messages
teacher-dashboard.htmlSubject, class and semester steps; student filter; batch grade entry with validation; printable report cards with automatic averages
viewer-dashboard.htmlRead-only student search, profiles, academic records, financial statements and reports with bar charts

Main features

  • Students: register, view, edit and delete; duplicate IDs are blocked; the ID format is KC plus five digits; editing an ID updates that student’s grades and payments too.
  • Grades: three marks per subject (Test 1, Test 2, Exam). The subject average, letter grade and overall average are calculated automatically. Marks must be from 0 to 100, and one bad mark blocks the whole batch.
  • Tuition: balance = tuition due − total paid. The status is Paid (nothing left), Pending (balance left, due date not passed) or Outstanding (balance left, due date passed). Over-payments and future payment dates are rejected.
  • Roles: Administrator, Teacher and General Authorized User. Each dashboard checks the role before it shows anything. The viewer’s script has no code that writes data.
  • Design: red, blue and white school colours, dashboard cards, organised tables, CSS Grid and Flexbox layouts, a phone menu and media queries at 900px, 768px and 480px, and a print stylesheet for report cards.

Limits of simulated front-end authentication

  • The usernames and passwords are saved in the browser as plain text. Anyone can read them with the developer tools.
  • The dashboards trust the role saved in the browser. Someone who edits browser storage could change their own role.
  • There is no password hashing, no lock-out after wrong attempts and no secure session token.
  • Real systems check passwords on a server, store them hashed, use HTTPS and keep the role on the server.

10. How the data is stored

Storage keyWhat it holds
knox_studentsStudent profiles, class, semester, tuition due and due date
knox_teachers, knox_subjects, knox_classesTeacher directory, subjects with their teacher, class names
knox_gradesTest 1, Test 2 and Exam marks for each student, subject and semester
knox_paymentsEach tuition payment with date, amount and method
knox_announcements, knox_messagesHome page announcements and Contact Us messages
knox_users (localStorage), knox_session (sessionStorage)Sample accounts and the logged-in user

The first time any page opens, sample records are added. After that, the Administrator’s changes are saved with JSON.stringify and read back with JSON.parse. The browser keeps this data per website address, so each person who opens the hosted site gets their own private copy.

11. Testing

The project was tested with scripts written during development. They ran every dashboard in a simulated browser and in Google Chrome. The checks covered navigation and links, login and logout, role protection, student create, edit and delete, duplicate IDs, form validation, grade entry, report card calculations, tuition calculations and status changes, search and filtering, read-only access, saved data after a refresh, phone-width layout, and console errors. All checks passed, and Chrome reported no console errors or warnings. The separate Testing Checklist lists every check and leaves boxes for you to repeat the important ones in your own browser. Firefox, Safari, real phones and physical printing were not tested.
