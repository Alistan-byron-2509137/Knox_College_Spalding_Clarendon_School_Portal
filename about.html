About Knox College

History, academics, student life, facilities and the people who teach here.

School history

Knox College opened in 1947 in Spalding, Clarendon, on a hilltop close to the Clarendon–Manchester border. It was co-founded by Rev. Dr. Lewis Davidson and Mr. David Bent, and it is named after the Scottish reformer John Knox.

The school is part of the Knox complex of schools, which also includes the Neighbourhood Early Childhood Institute, Knox Junior School and Knox Community College. The complex is owned and administered by the United Church in Jamaica and the Cayman Islands, and each school has its own principal and board of management.

Knox College is a grant-aided, co-educational high school for day pupils and boarders. Public sources describe it as the only co-educational boarding school at the secondary level in Jamaica.

Academic information

Knox College teaches Grades 7 to 13. The school has up to seven streams, and it takes in about 300 students in Grade 7 each year and about 100 in Grade 13.

Work for the Caribbean Examinations Council (CXC) exams starts in Grade 9. Students sit CSEC at the end of Grade 11, and CAPE in Grades 12 and 13.

English Language, English Literature and Mathematics are compulsory. CSEC students take at least eight subjects.

A student service work programme, started in 1968, is part of the course. Students must complete at least 40 hours of it to earn the school diploma, and seniors complete at least 50 hours.

Student life and community

Student leadership

Students choose their council representatives, and the president and vice-president are chosen in a democratic election. Teachers select prefects for leadership, academic performance and attitude. The Head Boy and Head Girl come from the prefects.

Clubs and societies

About thirty clubs include cadets, drama, debating, music, Junior Achievement, 4-H, Red Cross, journalism, sign language, science and technology, Spanish, and the Duke of Edinburgh Award.

Sports

Basketball, volleyball, chess, netball, cricket, football, lawn tennis, table tennis, badminton, swimming and track and field.

Houses and colours

The four houses are Forbes Dennis, Grant, Murray and Webster. The school colours are red, blue and white.

Achievements

  • Knox College has won several ISSA basketball and volleyball championships.
  • The school takes part in national and regional competitions in science, Spanish, literacy, visual and performing arts, Junior Achievement and 4-H.
  • About 300 students were in the graduating class of 2026, according to a Jamaica Information Service report.
  • News reports list Rear Admiral Antonette Wemyss Gorman, Jamaica's Chief of Defence Staff, among Knox College's past students.

Facilities and campus

The campus covers about 100 acres in Spalding. It includes boarding dormitories, a playing field and a gymnasium.

In March 2022 the school broke ground on a J$200 million sixth-form block because more students were enrolling in the sixth-form programme. In July 2026 the Ministry of Education announced over J$319 million for rehabilitation work at the school after Hurricane Melissa.

Admissions

Knox College welcomes day pupils and boarders. Most students come from nearby districts and towns, and many come from other parishes across Jamaica.

For the current admission steps, fees and boarding places, contact the school office. The phone numbers and address are on the Contact Us page.

Why I chose Knox College

I spent five years at Knox College as a boarder, and it became a big part of my childhood. Living on campus made school more than classes. It was home, routine and community, and it let me get to know leaders like our Principal, Mr. Bourne, and our Vice Principal, Mrs. Platt.

I studied IT, Electrical, Mathematics, English Language, English Literature, Spanish and French. Mr. Brown taught me Mathematics, Miss Black taught English Language, Miss Williams taught English Literature, and Miss Gray taught IT.

In every class I was the youngest student, so I made a habit of giving each lesson my best. Along the way I served on a committee on the school board and was nominated for Head Boy. I came second, and I learned that trying your best and serving others matters more than any title.

The school motto, to strive to serve and not to yield, still guides me as I study Computing today.

— Alistan Byron, former Knox College student and developer of this portal
